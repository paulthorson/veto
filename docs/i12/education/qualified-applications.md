# Qualified applications: apply to fewer roles, better

_The annual roadmap's thesis in one line: optimize for qualified conversations, not application volume. Never optimize applications-per-day._

## The core mistake

Most job seekers treat applications as lottery tickets: more tickets, more chances. Veto's design bet is that the opposite holds — every application costs attention (tailoring, follow-up, interview prep), and a scattershot history makes your positioning blurrier, not stronger. Veto is deliberately designed to discourage volume: the risk check is proposal-only by construction — there is no code path from it to an application — and nothing in the product optimizes applications-per-day.

## What "qualified" means

A qualified application is one where, before you apply, you can answer yes to all four:

1. **Fit evidence exists.** At least three posting requirements map to real facts in your history — not keywords, facts with dates and outcomes.
2. **No blockers.** Location, seniority, work authorization, and must-have skills clear. One honest blocker beats ten hopeful maybes.
3. **The posting is legible.** The JD decoder shows concrete responsibilities and a transparency score (0–100) backed by quoted evidence — one you can defend. Vague postings with no salary range aren't automatically bad — but they demand a clarifying question before you invest.
4. **You want the work.** Not the logo, not the salary band — the actual day-to-day described in the responsibilities section.

## A practical weekly loop

1. **Decode before you desire.** Paste the JD into the decoder. Read the quotes, not just the verdict.
2. **Run the risk check.** Treat warnings as interview questions, blockers as stop signs.
3. **Write the evidence map.** For each requirement: supported fact, gap, or question. Gaps become either grill questions or reasons to pass.
4. **Tailor from evidence.** Every tailored claim traces to an approved evidence item. If you can't source it, don't claim it.
5. **Record the outcome.** Applied, replied, screened, interviewed, offered, rejected — each as an explicit event. Fit scores don't learn on their own (the scorer stays deterministic), but a written record lets you see which signals predicted real outcomes; undocumented applications teach nothing.

## Red flags in your own process

- Applying to "see what happens" as a habit (a couple of times a month is already a pattern worth noticing).
- Rewriting your resume's job titles to match the posting (that's fabrication, not tailoring).
- Skipping the risk check because you're excited about the company.
- Measuring your week in applications sent instead of qualified conversations started.
