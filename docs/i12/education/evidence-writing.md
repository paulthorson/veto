# Evidence writing: turn history into sourced claims

_Every tailored statement in Veto traces to an approved evidence item. This guide shows how to write evidence worth tracing to._

## Claim vs. evidence

- **Claim:** "Strong leadership skills." (Unverifiable. Every candidate writes this.)
- **Evidence:** "Led a 6-engineer team shipping payments migration, 2023–2024; cut p95 latency 40%; two reports promoted." (Checkable. Specific. Dated.)

Recruiters pattern-match claims; hiring managers interrogate evidence. Write for the interrogation.

## The anatomy of a good evidence item

1. **Situation in one line.** Team size, scope, timeframe.
2. **Your action, specifically.** What *you* did, not what the team did. "Designed the rollout plan" beats "was involved in rollout."
3. **A measured outcome.** Numbers where they exist; honest qualitative outcomes where they don't. Never invent metrics — a missing number is better than a fabricated one.
4. **A source.** The role, project, or artifact it came from. Veto stores this provenance; you should be able to too.

## Reusable phrasing without lying

Keep an evidence library: approved one-liners tied to source facts. Reuse the phrasing across applications — that's efficiency. What you must not do is stretch one evidence item to cover a requirement it doesn't support. The fit decoder marks those as gaps; respect the gaps.

## Handling thin areas

- **Career switchers:** transferable evidence counts — "ran incident response for a 50-person support org" is leadership evidence even if the domain changed. Say the domain changed; don't hide it.
- **Junior candidates:** project and coursework evidence is real evidence. Scope it honestly ("course project, 4 people, 8 weeks").
- **Gaps in employment:** don't narrate them on the resume unless asked. Have a one-line honest answer ready for interviews.

## What Veto checks

The honesty layer verifies that tailored claims stay within the bounds of approved evidence. If a draft claims something your evidence doesn't support, that's a finding, not a suggestion — fix the claim or add the evidence, never the reverse.
