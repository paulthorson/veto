# Interview preparation: practice against your actual gaps

_Generic interview advice is cheap. Our bet: preparation driven by your target role's gaps — observed across practice sessions — is the preparation most likely to move outcomes. That's a design bet, not a proven claim: the tools can't predict hiring outcomes, so treat this as a hypothesis to test against your own results._

## Start from the gap, not the question list

Before collecting "top 50 interview questions," get your gap list:

1. Decode the JD and map requirements to your evidence.
2. Mark each requirement: supported, gap, or question.
3. Your prep plan is the gaps, in priority order. Everything else is warm-up.

## The story format that works

For behavioral questions, use evidence you already wrote (see [Evidence writing](evidence-writing.md)):

- **Situation** (15 seconds): enough context to follow, no org-chart tourism.
- **Complication** (15 seconds): what made it hard. This is the part most candidates skip — and it's what interviewers score.
- **Action** (45 seconds): your specific decisions and trade-offs.
- **Result** (15 seconds): measured outcome, plus what you'd do differently.

Ninety seconds per story. If it runs longer, you're narrating, not answering.

## Practice modes worth your time

- **Recruiter screen:** 30-second positioning + comp expectations + logistics. Rehearse the summary until it's boring.
- **Hiring manager:** depth on 2–3 evidence items. They will probe the complication — have it ready.
- **Peer/technical:** trade-offs, not trivia. "Why this approach over that one" beats reciting definitions.
- **Adversarial:** have someone attack your weakest gap directly. Uncomfortable; highest ROI.

## Track what improved

After each session, note which rubric dimension moved (structure, evidence, clarity, trade-offs, question quality) and which didn't. A dimension scoring below bar in two of three sessions is an observed gap — drill it specifically instead of doing another generic mock.

## Questions to ask them

Prepare five; you'll use three. At least one should come from your JD decode ("The posting mentions on-call but not rotation size — how is that structured?"). Questions sourced from the decode signal diligence; generic questions signal you didn't read the posting.
